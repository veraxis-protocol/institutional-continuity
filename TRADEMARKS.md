# Trademark and Conformance-Designation Policy

## Open category vocabulary

The project wants the technical community to be able to discuss and implement the category without asking permission to use ordinary descriptive vocabulary.

Descriptive use of terms such as:

- **Institutional Continuity Infrastructure**
- **ICI**
- the canonical continuity chain;
- join identifiers **J1–J8**;
- public status vocabulary;
- public invariant identifiers;

is encouraged for accurate technical discussion, interoperability, scholarship, comparison, and implementation mapping, subject to applicable law and without implying endorsement.

## Veraxis marks

Names and identifiers associated specifically with Veraxis products, services, organizations, or source-origin claims—including **Veraxis**, **Veraxis.One**, and Veraxis product names—may be protected trademarks or trade names.

This repository's copyright licenses do not grant a trademark license beyond uses required to identify the origin of licensed material or uses otherwise permitted by law.

## Reserved conformance designations

The following are examples of **reserved program designations** and must not be used to imply verified conformance unless and until the applicable program authorizes that use:

- `ICI Conformant`
- `ICTS Verified`
- `Veraxis Verified`
- substantially confusing variants used as badges, seals, certification labels, or product claims.

These labels are program-governance terms. Their presence here does not state that a legal certification-mark registration has issued.

## Acceptable use

Generally acceptable:

- “Our system maps to J3–J5 of the ICI model.”
- “This project implements the ICI Compatibility Profile format.”
- “We evaluated our system using ICTS vX.Y.”
- “Compatible with the open Institutional Continuity architecture,” when technically accurate and not presented as official certification.

Not acceptable without authorization:

- using a Veraxis logo as your product logo;
- implying sponsorship, partnership, certification, or approval that does not exist;
- presenting a self-run test as `ICTS Verified` if the applicable program requires independent evidence;
- modifying a reserved conformance badge in a way likely to confuse users.

## Forks and derivatives

Forks may accurately state their origin and may use open category vocabulary. They should choose a distinct project/product name when necessary to avoid confusion about maintenance, endorsement, or certification status.
